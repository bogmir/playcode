## Relations between plays (2026-10-26), after FileMaker parity

The project's next phase studies how Spanish plays circulated in Europe up to the mid-18th century, and needs more relations between plays than `relationship_type` and `parent_play_id` hold. Gathered from the research team's emails of 2026-10-24 to 26: question 15 amended, 40 answered, 53-57 open, in `docs/stakeholder/preguntas.html`. Development starts once Playcode does everything FileMaker does for EMOTHE (as the team was told); the design waits for 53-57.

- [ ] **Kinds and attributes of a relation** - the kinds: original; later version (the team's "original no principal", today `version`, question 40); translation; free translation (keeps the structure, departs from the sense systematically); adaptation (refundición is one with it: question 15 amended; the kind was dropped on 2026-10-31, `docs/history.md`); partial adaptation (takes a subplot or a sustained motif); a play with borrowings (situations, episodes, characters, ideas). Whether the two plays share a language (intra- or interlingual) follows from their `language`, never stored. An adaptation, whole or partial, names its source ("adaptación") or does not ("apropiación"); one into another language may also be a transformation or an imitation (question 55); borrowings perhaps the first pair (question 56). Every translation and adaptation is historical or modern, the editor's choice with no cut-off year, and the next phase studies the historical ones; neither Playcode nor the FileMaker export we have holds it. A play can have several sources, so `parent_play_id` gives way to a table of relations, each with its kind and attributes. Families are trees, not one level: Lope's *Don Lope de Cardona* (EMOTHE0236), Rotrou's adaptation of it (0687) and that adaptation's Spanish translation (0728); *Las mocedades del Cid*, *Le Cid* (0008) and *Le Cid*'s translations. Today `/plays` (`Catalogue.list_plays_grouped/1`) shows one level, so a third would be hidden (question 15's first migration made a cycle and a chain that hid five plays); the comparison (`PlayComparison.build_family/2`) gives a play its parent's family, not its own derived plays; the static site follows chains (`root_id/2`). Two plays from one source (*The Young Admiral*, 0237, and *Don Lope de Cardone*, both under 0236 already) are related through it, with no kind of their own. TEI: `<title type>` cannot hold several sources or the attributes, so `<relation>` elements in the header, the old `<title type>` still read; a row in `ida-y-vuelta.html` and the check in `complete_copy_test.exs`. The site's catalogue filters by kind and by historical or modern; the English names are question 54
- [ ] **Plays without a text** (question 57) - the emails say Playcode holds source plays, and their translations and adaptations, with no text. Today every play comes from a TEI or Word file with its text; the play page, the downloads, the comparison, the statistics and the static site all assume one, and such a play's TEI file would be a header with an empty body.
