Variant readings and HTML fragments in the TEI files

Six plays carry a critical apparatus, the readings of other witnesses beside the edited text, and seven have pieces of HTML typed into the text. Playcode currently pastes both into the lines, so readers see them as words. This page shows what the old site did with them and what we plan. The questions they raise are on the project’s list of questions.

Prepared by Bogdan, 10 October 2026, revised 11 October, from the 370 TEI files of the published corpus and the pages of emothe.uv.es.

Variant readings

An entry follows the word it is about and lists which witness reads what: the reading the edition follows, the other readings, and sometimes the editor’s comment. 604 entries in six plays:

Old site: “mucho la hermosura llama*,” and the asterisk opens: – H llama (Corrección de Hartzenbusch, lección que acepto por ajustarse mejor a la rima consonante y al sentido del pasaje.) – P3 falta – MP infama

Playcode today: “mucho la hermosura llama llamafalta infama ,”

  • The line keeps only the edited text, as on the old site.
  • Each entry becomes a note of its own kind, “Textual variant”, after the word it is about. Opening it lists each witness and its reading, then the comment, as the old site did.
  • Researchers can edit the readings in the content editor, like any note.
  • The TEI file comes back as it was: the export writes each entry back where it stood.

The old site showed only 4 of Hamlet’s 94 entries, and its Folio readings went through the HTML fragments below. Of the 94, 4 are copies inside the editor’s notes; 81 of the other 90 sit in the first 313 lines, and some are damaged (question 2).

HTML typed into the text

Seven plays hold 1,211 fragments of HTML, written as text inside the TEI. The old site printed them as HTML, and its stylesheet hid one kind (.folio { display: none }). Playcode stores them as text, so 236 lines show the tags themselves. They do four different things:
