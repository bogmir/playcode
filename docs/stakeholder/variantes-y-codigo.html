Variant readings and HTML fragments in the TEI files

Six plays carry a critical apparatus, the readings of other witnesses beside the edited text, and seven have pieces of HTML typed into the text. Playcode currently pastes both into the lines, so readers see them as words. This page shows what the old site did with them, what we plan, and the questions only the project can answer.

Prepared by Bogdan, 10 October 2026, from the 370 TEI files of the published corpus and the pages of emothe.uv.es.

Playcode today: “mucho la hermosura llama llamafalta infama ,”

  • The line keeps only the edited text, as on the old site.
  • Each entry becomes a note of its own kind, “Textual variant”, after the word it is about. Opening it lists each witness and its reading, then the comment, as the old site did.
  • Researchers can edit the readings in the content editor, like any note.
  • The TEI file comes back as it was: the export writes each entry back where it stood.

The old site showed only 4 of Hamlet’s 94 entries; its Folio readings went through the HTML fragments below.

HTML typed into the text

Variant readings

1

Should a variant be marked with a number, like the notes, or with an asterisk as before?

The old site marked a variant with an asterisk and listed it apart from the notes, which carry numbers. Playcode numbers the notes 1, 2, 3 through the play. A variant could take the next number, so a page has one sequence and one list, or keep a sign of its own.

Answer

2

Should Hamlet show all 94 entries in its TEI file?

The old site showed 4 of them. The others include, at v. 8, “Barnardo.” where the Folio reads “Barnardo?”, and readings with the original spelling, such as F1 “euen” (mod. “even”).

Answer

3

How should the witnesses be named?

The files name them with the editor’s sigla: Q2, F1, Aut., PXXIV, H, P3, MP, Dyce. Only Everyman lists its witnesses in full; for the others the files do not say which edition H or MP is.

Answer

4

Should readers see the variants by default?

The reading pages have switches for line numbers, stage directions and asides. Variants could be shown with a switch to hide them, hidden with a switch to show them, or always shown.

Answer

HTML typed into the text

5

What should readers of Hamlet see of the words found only in the Folio?

There are 247 such passages, from a single word (“O God, O God,”) to whole speeches. The old site hid them, so its text was Q2’s.

Answer

6

How should the Q4 Additions of The Spanish Tragedy be marked?

The English, French, Italian and Spanish versions mark where each addition starts and ends with a superscript Q4; the Spanish one numbers them (Q4.1…). Some ends are written backwards, which on the old site set the following text in superscript.

Answer

7

Should the “Aside” and “Exit” labels of The Rover appear in the text?

There are 40. The old site hid them; asides are marked anyway.

Answer

8

Is v. 623 of Mariamne “Devant le Ciel voûté, trône du Dieu qui tonne,”?

The file has “tr”, a stray superscript tag, then “ne”. On the old site the rest of the line ran on in superscript.
