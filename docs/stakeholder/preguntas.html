Variant readings

Six plays carry a critical apparatus: 604 entries, each after the word it is about, listing which witness reads what. The old site showed the edited text with an asterisk that opened the readings. Playcode currently pastes the readings into the line, so La discreta enamorada, v. 72 reads “mucho la hermosura llama llamafalta infama ,”. We plan to keep only the edited text in the line and make each entry a note of its own kind, “Textual variant”.

The full background, with the six plays and examples: Variant readings and HTML fragments in the TEI files.

1

Should a variant be marked with a number, like the notes, or with an asterisk as before?

The old site marked a variant with an asterisk and listed it apart from the notes, which carry numbers. Playcode numbers the notes 1, 2, 3 through the play. A variant could take the next number, so a page has one sequence and one list, or keep a sign of its own.

2

Should Hamlet show all 94 entries in its TEI file?

The old site showed 4 of them. The others include, at v. 8, “Barnardo.” where the Folio reads “Barnardo?”, and readings with the original spelling, such as F1 “euen” (mod. “even”).

Answer

3

How should the witnesses be named?

The files name them with the editor’s sigla: Q2, F1, Aut., PXXIV, H, P3, MP, Dyce. Only Everyman lists its witnesses in full; for the others the files do not say which edition H or MP is. Playcode now lists each play’s witnesses as FileMaker holds them, so a siglum can point at one.

Answer

4

Should readers see the variants by default?

The reading pages have switches for line numbers, stage directions and asides. Variants could be shown with a switch to hide them, hidden with a switch to show them, or always shown.

Answer

HTML typed into the text

Seven plays hold 1,211 fragments of HTML written as text inside the TEI. The old site printed them as HTML, and its stylesheet hid one kind. Playcode stores them as text, so 236 lines show the tags themselves. Whatever you choose, these tags will no longer reach the page as text.

Background and examples: HTML typed into the text.

Line breaks and prose line numbers

The TEI files mark a line break inside a paragraph with <lb/>. They use it in three ways. The old site honoured all three. Playcode currently turns every one into a space.

A numbered break: where a printed line of the source edition starts. 9

How should prose line numbers be shown?

40 plays number their prose lines as the source edition prints them: Hamlet, The Way of the World, Endymion, The Malcontent, King Lear among them. The numbers would follow the “Line numbers” switch, as verse numbers do.

Answer

10

Should the plain line breaks and the empty lines show, as on the old site?

They shape letters, songs and quoted verse inside speeches, and set a dedication’s signature apart. Running them on, as Playcode does now, joins lines that were meant to stand apart.

Answer

Historical performances

Next on the list is bringing over FileMaker’s historical performances, which emothe.uv.es prints under “Representaciones antiguas”: company, venue, date, cast, venue type, a note and the information source. FileMaker holds 317 performances, 243 of them on 59 plays Playcode publishes.

  • 252 performances cite one of 104 sources. Wiggins’s British Drama 1533–1642 alone is 81 of them, cited 152 times: a record per citation, most with their own pages, under ten spellings of the title (“1532-1642”, “1533+1642”, “Volume IX”…).
  • CATCOM is cited 44 times, and its licence asks for its credit line wherever it is cited.
  • The performance’s place is not in the tables we have; it is in the request at the end of this page.
11

Should a performance’s information source be a work from the bibliography?

If it is, there is one list of works: British Drama, volume 6, is one entry, and each performance gives its own pages, as a play’s bibliography already gives its own volume and pages. Correcting the entry corrects every performance that cites it, and the citation prints the same everywhere. A separate list of sources keeps performances apart from the bibliography but holds a second copy of the same works. Free text is what FileMaker prints, and a correction then means editing each performance. We recommend the first.

Answer

12

Should a work cited only for a performance also appear in the play’s bibliography?

For example, a play whose only mention of Wiggins is as the source of a performance. It could be listed beside the performance alone, or also among the play’s critical studies.

Answer

Witnesses

Playcode now lists each play’s witnesses, the manuscripts and early printings, as emothe.uv.es prints its “Testimonios”: 508 witnesses on 106 plays, brought over from FileMaker.

13

Are the 11 witnesses FileMaker attributes to Étienne Jodelle mistakes?

They belong to six Spanish plays: La verdad sospechosa, Los locos de Valencia, El anzuelo de Fenisa, Adonis y Venus, El bastardo Mudarra and La discreta enamorada. Jodelle is the first name in FileMaker’s list of attributions, so a form left at its first choice would name him. Playcode prints these 11 with no attribution; on Jodelle’s own plays his name stays.

Answer

What should these eleven kinds of note be called?

Six kinds have a name: translator’s, editor’s, critical editor’s, digital editor’s, author’s, and a plain note. These eleven all show as a plain “Note”, and the Notes page’s filter lumps them together. Most are subjects rather than who wrote the note. Give each a name, or say which should merge into another (the digital editor’s note into “Digital editor’s note”, say).

FileMaker tables we still need

Not questions, but they unblock the next pieces of work. One export can carry all of them.

  • The places tables: T10, the T10.11 tables (city, continent, vague location, country, region) and T10.3_Region. They give each play its places of action, and very likely the place of a performance: 142 performances point at a place table that is not in the export.
  • The genre table, T01.42, the genre hierarchy, instead of the value lists.
  • When exporting, choose every field (“Mover todo”). The export dialog remembers the previous selection, which is how one table first arrived with 26 of its 41 fields.
