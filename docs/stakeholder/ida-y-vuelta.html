Where each part of a play goes

A play in Playcode reaches six places. This page shows which parts of a play reach which, and where something is lost on the way, above all between a play and its TEI file.

Prepared by Bogdan. Measured on 11 October 2026 on the 394 plays of the development copy.

Why the TEI file matters

The TEI file is meant to be a complete copy of a play. It is published with the site as the edition's long-term archive, and it is how a play moves: a backup restored, a play loaded into another installation. If a play is exported and its file imported into an empty Playcode, everything should come back.

To check, every play was exported and its file imported again as a new play, then the two were compared field by field. The text comes back whole. Several things around it do not.

Importing a file over the play it came from is a different case. That keeps what the team has set in Playcode (whether the play is complete, its family, its historical time) and everything brought in from FileMaker, so it loses much less. What it replaces is the text itself.

The six places

Edited
A form in Playcode’s admin pages where the team can change it.
Written to TEI
In the XML file Playcode exports, the one published with the site.
Read from TEI
Back in Playcode when that file is imported into an installation that does not have the play.
Downloads
The HTML, PDF and EPUB a reader can download.
Play page
The play’s page in Playcode itself.
Published site
The static edition published on emothe.uv.es.

Notes

  1. One attribution, “fiable” on The Shy Courtier (EMOTHE0653), does not come back.
  2. The downloads print the title, author, editors, sources, dedications and cast list; the EPUB also the original title and the licence. The publication details, the project and editorial statements and the identifiers are not printed.
  3. The file says a play is a translation, but not which play it translates: 153 plays come back without their original. An adaptation or a refundición would lose even the kind of relationship (none is recorded yet).
  4. Whether a play is complete is not in the file, so an imported play arrives as a draft. All 373 complete plays would be hidden from visitors and left off the published site until someone marked them complete again.
  5. The date is written only when it has years. A dating given only in words, such as “posterior 1590 y anterior 1595”, is left out: 18 plays.
  6. 109 plays, 38 of them with an explanatory note.
  7. No play has its form set by hand yet. Playcode works it out from the text, which does come back.
  8. They come back marked as coming from the file, whatever their origin. Two digital editors of the same play come back as “editor” (3 plays), and one source loses its role “canon60” (EMOTHE0727).
  9. All 510 witnesses of 107 plays come back, but without the FileMaker record number that ties each one to FileMaker, and line breaks inside a title or a note become spaces (25 fields in 9 plays).
  10. The bibliography is written to the file in full, as structured TEI, but Playcode does not read it back: 2,804 references on 115 plays are lost. The researchers’ private notes are never written, on purpose, because the file is published with the site.
  11. Checked by an automated test. No play in the development copy has places yet, so the measurement could not count them.
  12. The file holds one cast list, with no heading. 319 plays lose the heading (“Hablan en ella las personas siguientes”, “DRAMATIS PERSONAE”), and the plays with a cast list per act or part (La Tragédie espagnole, ’Tis Pity She’s a Whore, El galán Castrucho, Le Malade imaginaire) come back with a single list. The play page and the downloads print each cast list with its heading, but every one lists all the characters; the published site prints one list without a heading.
  13. Not editable in Playcode yet; both come from the TEI files.
  14. The formula that closes an act or a play (“FIN DEL PRIMER ACTO”, “Terminat hora diem; terminat author opus”) is in the file and on the play page, but on no page of the published site and in none of the downloads: 224 of them in 131 plays.
  15. Two italic passages side by side come back as one, and an empty one in a note is dropped. The text reads the same (10 places).
  16. Worked out from the text each time, so not part of the file.

What happens next

  1. Read the bibliography back from the file. The largest loss, and the next piece of work.
  2. Write the rest into the file: complete or draft, the play a translation translates, the historical time, a dating given in words, the cast list’s heading and a play’s several cast lists, and each witness’s FileMaker record number.
  3. Show the closing formulas on the published site and in the downloads.
  4. An automated check that exports a play with every part filled in, imports it into an empty Playcode and compares, so that nothing added later can be lost the same way.

None of this needs a decision from the team. What the source TEI files hold that Playcode does not read at all (the critical apparatus, line breaks, HTML typed into the text) is a separate list, in the questions for the project.
