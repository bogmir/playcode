Moving each play’s bibliography out of FileMaker

Playcode will replace FileMaker as the place where each play’s bibliography is kept. On 1 October we received an export of the bibliography tables themselves, so we can now see the records rather than the printed lines on the website. This page sets out what that export answered, what is still missing, and the questions that remain.

Prepared by Bogdan. First version 25 September 2026, updated 1–2 October 2026 with the exports of the BibliografiaSelecta tables from ctce_dades.

What the new export answered

  • The records are structured. Each one keeps the article or chapter (author, title) apart from the book or journal it appears in (author, title), with year, volume, pages, a web address, a note, and codes for category, type and language. That fits Playcode and the TEI files directly.
  • It is the same data the website shows. The export holds 2,640 records and 2,658 links to plays. On 101 of the 106 plays with a published criticism list, it reproduces that list exactly.
  • Language code 5 is Portuguese. The full list is castellano, francés, inglés, italiano, portugués, alemán.
  • There are ten record types. Seven are in use; prólogo, homenaje and colección have no records yet.
  • There is no “selected” checkbox. The 698 unprinted records reported in the first version of this page came from an out-of-date search field, not from real records. What is really unpublished is smaller: 118 records added after the website was last generated, and 86 records with no category.
  • Lists run newest first, by year. 99 of 102 published lists follow that rule exactly, so there is no hand-made order to preserve.
  • Cities and publishers resolve. The city and publisher tables sent later on 1 October name every city and all but one publisher used by the bibliography. One record has a publisher name typed where its number should be.
  • Nothing else is linked. The relationship graph shows that the bibliography depends only on its own tables, cities and publishers.
  • The modern editions arrived too. 985 editions and 968 links to plays, matching the website’s list exactly on 118 of 120 plays. Each link also records where the play sits inside the edition (volume and pages), and editions carry their siglum, such as ARD3Q2 or RSC. With this, everything the bibliography pages show is in hand.
  • The records are complete. The full export of the bibliography table (2 October) holds every translator, editor, journal issue and original title the website prints. Thank you.
  • Five plays already in Playcode gain a bibliography (EMOTHE0659, 0670, 0749, 0777 and 0779). The website export never listed them, but the tables do.

The year field also holds a few things that are not years: page ranges such as “121-123”, “[s.a.]” and “letra del s. XIX”. Playcode keeps the text as typed and sorts on the first four-digit year it finds.

What we plan to do

  • Each play gets a bibliography in four groups: modern editions, criticism, translations and adaptations. Researchers can add, correct and delete entries in Playcode.
  • Modern editions keep their siglum, series and reference, and each play keeps its own volume and pages inside the edition.
  • Entries keep FileMaker’s fields separate and remember the FileMaker record they came from. Re-running the import adds new records and updates untouched ones, but never overwrites an entry someone has edited in Playcode.
  • Once the missing tables arrive, Playcode prints each citation from its fields. Empty fields are left out, so the “{Falta …}” placeholders disappear. Until then, the citation FileMaker prints is kept for display.
  • Lists run newest first. Entries without a year go last.
  • The bibliography is also written into each play’s TEI file, in the back matter, with the article and the book or journal as separate levels. It stays out of <sourceDesc>, which describes the sources of the edition itself.

Questions

Questions 1 to 3 are about the FileMaker data and may need whoever exported the tables. Questions 4 to 7 are editorial choices. Your answers are kept in this browser as you type. Send them with the button at the end.

About the FileMaker database

1

What are the 86 records without a category?

76 of them are linked to plays, 35 to EMOTHE0659 alone, but without a category they never reach the website. Some look like ordinary criticism, for example “Smith, Peter, and Nigel Wood. Hamlet: Theory in Practice. Buckingham: Open University Press, 1996.”

Answer

2

Are the 118 records added since the website was last generated ready to publish?

The same goes for 78 modern-edition links, on 10 plays. The bibliography records sit on 10 plays, for example La place Royale (22), El desdén con el desdén (18) and Le menteur (15). As far as we can tell, they are missing from the website only because it has not been regenerated since they were added.

Answer

3

Can the broken links and unlinked records be dropped?

In the bibliography, 74 links have no record, 19 point to a record that no longer exists, and 102 records are linked to no play. In the modern editions, 7 links are broken and 129 editions are linked to no play. We plan to skip the broken links. The unlinked records could still be useful later.

Answer

Editorial choices

4

Should a translation also show the original play’s bibliography?

FileMaker links records to each version separately. 30 records are shared between two or three versions, and the rest belong to one. A reader of a translation would currently see only the records linked to that translation.

Answer

5

What should Playcode do with the notes?

178 records have a note and only 3 are printed. Most are working notes, such as “Sobre: <<Adonis y Venus>>…” or “Toma ejemplos de: <<El sembrar en buena tierra>>…”. A few hold a web address or a publisher typed in the wrong place.

Answer

6

Should lists stay newest first?

That is what FileMaker does today, computed from the year. If curators want their own order instead, Playcode needs a way to rearrange lists that can run to 62 entries, the length of Hamlet’s criticism.

Answer

7

Should one correction to a shared edition reach every play?

23 modern editions sit under several plays, up to eleven: the Wells and Taylor Complete Works, Drama of the English Renaissance II, English Drama 1580-1642. We plan to keep one copy per play, each with its own volume and pages, and to remember which copies came from the same FileMaker record. If corrections to these editions are common, a single edit should reach all of them.

Answer
