Prologues on the wrong side

The list questions 31 and 59 promised. By your rule, a prologue spoken on the stage is part of the play’s text, and one printed for readers is a text around it. Here is every prologue that sits on the other side, how we read each one, and the texts for the reader whose kind looks doubtful. Confirm or correct each line; nothing changes in the files until you do.

Prepared by Bogdan, 3 November 2026, from every play in Playcode and its TEI file. The questions: 31, 59 and 66.

What we checked

Every prologue already in a play’s text, 40 of them, is spoken: each has its speeches or its lines. Three hold a line for readers at their head, and stay as they are: El mercader amante’s loa (EMOTHE0659) opens with its dedication to don Tomás de Vilanova, Le Malade imaginaire’s prologue (EMOTHE0714) with Molière’s note on the King’s victories, and The Malcontent’s (EMOTHE0363, and its Italian and Spanish) with Marston’s motto. Of the 80 texts filed as prologues around the play, 45 look spoken, in 33 plays; one more stands after the play. Every dedication, 85 of them, is before the play, where it belongs: none is on the wrong side.
