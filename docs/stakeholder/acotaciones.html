Studying a play’s stage directions

Three ways the published site could help a reader who studies the stage directions themselves: where the entrances and exits fall, how the stage business is written, how often asides are marked. Each is drawn in the site’s own colours with the first scene of Hamlet.

Today the site can only show or hide the stage directions. An act page holds about 23 of them (54 on a busy one, 232 at most, La Tempête, act 1); a whole play about 120, 440 at most (Le Roi Lear). Three in four stand on a line of their own, one in four inside a verse or a speech. The places and kinds in the pictures are illustrative. Back to question 52

A Arrows in the side panel

Under “Show: Stage directions”, two arrows step through the stage directions of the page you are reading, and the one you are on is shaded. emothe.uv.es did the same for split verses.

William Shakespeare

The Tragedy of Hamlet, Prince of Denmark

Act 1, Scene 1

Enter Barnardo and Francisco, two sentinels.

Barnardo

Who’s there?

Francisco

Nay, answer me. Stand and unfold yourself.

Barnardo

Well, good night.
If you do meet Horatio and Marcellus,
The rivals of my watch, bid them make haste.

Enter Horatio and Marcellus.

Francisco

I think I hear them. Stand ho, who is there?

For it

  • The smallest change; nothing new on the page
  • Familiar to emothe.uv.es’s readers

Against it

  • One stage direction at a time: they cannot be read together or compared
  • Counts only the page you are on; on a phone the side panel is folded away at the top

B A study mode

A “Study stage directions” switch fades the speeches, as Highlight does for a character today, and opens a bar along the bottom of the text: where you are, the stage direction itself, the arrows, and the keys J and K. The bar stays in reach on a phone.

For it

  • Each stage direction seen in its place, the rest of the text quiet around it
  • Always in reach, on a phone too

Against it

  • Still one at a time, and only the page you are on
  • Needs the browser’s scripts; nothing in a print

C A page of the play’s stage directions

Every stage direction of the play on one page, in order, act by act and scene by scene, as the Notes page lists the notes. Each one’s place links to it in the text, where it is shaded. It can be filtered by where it stands and, where the file says, by its kind.
