Verse, prose or both in the TEI files

Every EMOTHE file says whether its play is in verse, in prose or in both. Playcode does not read what the file says: it works the form out from the text. On 91 plays the two disagree. This page shows what the files say, what Playcode does, and the other place TEI offers for the label. The decisions it raises are questions 22 and 23 on the project’s list of questions.

Prepared by Bogdan, 13 October 2026, from the 372 TEI files of the published corpus and the plays of Playcode’s development copy.

What the files say

The label sits on extent, the line of the header that gives the size of the text, next to a count of verses or paragraphs:

<extent ana="prosa">52 párrafos</extent>

What Playcode does

  • Importing a file, it reads only the number and ignores the label.
  • It works the form out from the text: a play with one numbered verse line is verse, any other play prose. Only a person in Playcode can call a play “verse and prose”, and no one has yet.
  • Writing a file, it labels every play verso, with its count of numbered verse lines, so a prose play comes out as <extent ana="verso">0 versos</extent>.
  • The play page and the published site show the form Playcode holds, “Verse · 3021 verses” or “Prose”; the downloads print the one worked out from the text, never one set by hand.

Labelled verse, shown as prose: 17 plays

Seven have no verse line at all. Ten have verse lines that carry no number, which Playcode does not count. Three of those hold over a thousand verse lines each: As You Like It (EMOTHE0151, 1,235 verse lines), El caballero de la ardiente mano (EMOTHE0444, 1,377) and Una dama asesinada con la bondad (EMOTHE0496, 1,859). Two places TEI offers

On extent, as the files do now

Valid TEI, but the project’s own convention: extent is meant for the size of a text, and ana for a pointer to an interpretation declared elsewhere in the file, so a program reading TEI does not know what ana="verso" means.

In textClass, TEI’s classification of a text

Where the files already give their CDU number. The three forms are declared once in the header, beside the CDU, and each play points at one of them; extent keeps only the count. This is the place the TEI Guidelines give for classifying a text, and where other projects and tools look for it.

<classDecl>
  <taxonomy xml:id="CDU">…</taxonomy>
  <taxonomy xml:id="forma">
    <category xml:id="verso"><catDesc>Verso</catDesc></category>
    <category xml:id="prosa"><catDesc>Prosa</catDesc></category>
    <category xml:id="versoprosa"><catDesc>Verso y prosa</catDesc></category>
  </taxonomy>
</classDecl>
…
<textClass>
  <classCode scheme="#CDU">821.111-2</classCode>
  <catRef scheme="#forma" target="#versoprosa"/>
</textClass>
<extent>2639 versos</extent>

Either way, Playcode will read the label back, so a file it writes and imports again keeps its form, and the 41 plays in verse and prose come in as such.

Questions

Question 22, where the label belongs, was decided on 16 October 2026: on extent, as now, read and written back there. Question 23 asks whether the label or the text decides. Answer them on the project’s running list, Questions for the project, with the others.
